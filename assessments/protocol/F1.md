# F1 v2 — designated target-player acceptance

This revision replaces the first-exposure eligibility rule. The maintainer designates the actual target player; the stable public assessment alias is the manifest's F1 participant value. Keep the identity mapping in private assessment records. A different participant or an automated agent cannot substitute for this player's acceptance. Prior play, informal feedback and assessments under other bars do not disqualify the player. Returning sessions are expected while the builder improves.

Use the current playable build and record its displayed build identifier. Begin from an empty workshop; the player may use the in-game guide and controls. Observe without live coaching or doing construction steps for the player. Record elapsed active interaction time, what was built, whether it moved, any assistance and the player's actual feedback.

Pass requires all of the following:

- The designated player builds something that moves within 15 minutes without live assistance.
- The player explicitly accepts the basic building experience: selection, movement, editing and wiring are understandable enough to use independently.
- The player wants to build another machine.

Feedback may be a short written comment or voice comment; do not require a long questionnaire. Ask neutrally, and do not infer acceptance from silence, merely finishing a guide or an automated test. A session with unresolved material usability complaints does not pass. Record incomplete evidence as incomplete rather than manufacturing a verdict. Preserve previous feedback and failures; do not relabel historical sessions as current-build passes.

Inspect the build before another session: parts must be recognizable, connections must visibly meet their ports, and selection and editing must be legible. Automated walkthroughs can find defects but cannot provide the human verdict.

Assessment sessions remain append-only. The latest valid F1 assessment governs, including a newer failure from the same player. Source, served-build and protocol fingerprints must match. This change affects F1 eligibility and acceptance only; other human assessments and all physical, replay, performance, sandbox and final Course requirements remain in force.
